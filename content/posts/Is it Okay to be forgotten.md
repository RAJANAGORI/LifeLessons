Hmm. A tough question.  
  
I spent an hour thinking about this, observing people, and noticing how often we talk about things like these.  
  
Is it okay to be forgotten, or have we already been forgotten by everyone?  
  
Some will say it’s about priorities.  
Some will say it’s because of work.  
And some will simply ghost you.  
  
But where are we heading in life these days?  
  
We’re highly active on social media. We watch stories, smile at posts, stay updated on everyone’s lives, yet somehow, we don’t even have the courage to say a simple *“Hi.”*  
  
Of course, there are legitimate reasons for delayed replies. People have responsibilities, emotional limitations, and lives beyond their phones. Not every unanswered message means someone doesn’t care.  
  
But when a lack of effort becomes a consistent pattern, it’s understandable to question the connection.  
  
Why are we becoming like this? What’s happening to us?  
  
Look at the attitude these days. You receive a message, see it sitting in your inbox, and choose not to reply. Why? Because replying too quickly might make you look desperate or less important?  
  
Or perhaps you tell yourself, *“I’ll reply later. I promise.”* And then, somehow, later never comes.  
  
Do the countless notifications and unread messages on WhatsApp and other social media platforms give us a sense of importance? Do we feel more valued simply because so many people are trying to reach us? Does having a crowded inbox somehow make us feel like we’re the most important people on the planet?  
  
Or is it something else entirely?  
  
Sometimes, I wonder if we’ve become so accustomed to being connected digitally that we’ve forgotten how to connect emotionally.  
  
We’ve learned how to watch people’s lives without being a part of them, how to stay visible without being present, and how to know what everyone is doing without actually asking how they’re doing.  
  
And maybe that’s the saddest part of it all.  
  
Somewhere along the way, we’ve started confusing attention with affection, notifications with connection, and being remembered with being valued.  
  
So, after thinking about all this, I’ve come to a conclusion:  
  
**Maybe it’s literally okay to be forgotten.**  
  
Not because we don’t matter.  
Not because we don’t deserve to be remembered.  
But because we cannot keep expecting genuine connections from people who no longer wish to make an effort.  
  
Perhaps being forgotten is not always a loss. Sometimes, it’s a reminder to stop chasing attention, stop questioning our worth, and stop forcing ourselves into spaces where our presence no longer matters.  
  
After all, being remembered by everyone was never the goal.  
  
**Being genuinely remembered by a few people who truly care might be enough.**
