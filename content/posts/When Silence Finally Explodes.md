Being ruthless, powerful, and someone who always suppresses emotions - those are the most dangerous people I’ve ever seen. The word *emotion* itself is powerful. The ones who carry the most painful often appear to be the happiest.  
  
It’s like a bucket under a running tap - keep it flowing long enough, and it will overflow.  
  
Years of silence. Years of observation. Years of availability. Years of suppressed emotions. And then it all bursts out - shaking hands and legs, a heavy chest, irregular breathing, a racing heart. That lump in the throat, like a choking sensation, holds the person right at the edge of breaking.  
  
And the moment they finally break that moment is labeled as weakness in the crowd.  
  
While the body trembles like a volcano about to erupt, the mind spirals in every direction, pulling them into a hypnotic loop of constant self-doubt. A loss of control creeps in. What once felt real suddenly feels unreal.  
  
That weight of years feels like a massive stone - like something a thousand times heavier than the body itself. Like experiencing 100 G-force - enough to crush a person against whatever is holding them down. Enough to shut everything off in an instant. within a blink of an eye.   
  
And yet,   
  
when that person comes out of it, something changes. Not indestructible. But unshakable in a different way.  
  
They understand their own breaking point and that it didn’t destroy them. They don’t fear the fall anymore, because they’ve already survived it.  
  
The same emotions that once drowned them now become something they can carry. Control doesn’t come from suppression anymore but from awareness.  
  
They walk with a quiet confidence. Not loud, not forced real.  
  
Because once you’ve faced your own storm and made it through, you don’t see yourself as weak again.  
  
You see yourself as someone who endured.  
  
I’ve seen such people around me, I’ve observed them in different moods, mindsets, and situations. That constant clutch of engaging and disengaging the gears of emotion is far more complex than what the writer in me could describe above.
